Today's Paper Presentation:**AI Chains-Transparent and Controllable Human-AI Interaction by Chaining LLM Prompts**

This paper discusses some issues with Language Models (LLMs) and proposes a method called Chaining to address these problems. Currently, LLMs suffer from the **following issues**:

**Lack of Multi-step Reasoning Capability:**
LLMs may exhibit suboptimal performance when tasked with multi-step reasoning, even in handling complex problems, as they may engage in reasoning for only one step or a limited number of steps.

**Vulnerable to Exposure Bias:**
Exposure bias in LLMs, caused by their sequential autoregressive text generation, can result in accumulated errors and repetition, making them less effective in producing diverse and coherent text, especially in longer documents.

**Sensitive to Input Prompts:**
LLMs' sensitivity to input prompts can lead to variations in their performance under different prompts, causing instability.

To address these issues, the article introduces **the Chaining method**. Chaining **decomposes** complex multi-step reasoning problems into smaller, highly focused subproblems. Each subproblem is handled independently by dedicated LLMs, and the output from one step can **serve as input** for the next. This process is open to user intervention and modification, such as altering intermediate outputs, removing irrelevant information, or even redesigning the entire chain. This allows users to exert more flexible control over LLMs' outputs to achieve more satisfactory results.
