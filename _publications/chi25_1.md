title: "Raise Your Eyebrows: Investigating the Impact of Virtual Avatar’s Facial
Expression Scaling on Users’ Perception of Emotion and Uncanny Valley Effect
in Social Virtual Reality"

---

<i>Xueyang Wang<sup>*</sup>, <b>Sheng Zhao</b><sup>*</sup>, Yihe Wang, etc.</i>
<b>KeyWords</b>: Social Virtual Reality, Facial Expression Scaling, Cartoon Avatars, Emotion Perception, Uncanny Valley Effect, Mixed-Methods Study

Facial expression scaling, which artificially exaggerates or damps the amplitude of expression, has been shown to enhance expressiveness but risks triggering the uncanny valley effect. This study explores the effects of facial expression scaling on cartoon avatars in social VR using a mixed-methods approach with 30 participants. We assessed users’ emotional perception and uncanny valley responses while they viewed scaled, pre-recorded facial expressions in simulated face-to-face VR interactions. Results revealed that although expression exaggeration did not significantly influence the accuracy of emotion perception, it led to significant interaction effects with emotion categories on uncanny valley effect. Notably, exaggerated expressions both diminish or enhance the perceived realism, eeriness, and appeal of the expression, depending on the specific emotion conveyed. Joy and Surprise expressions, particularly in the mouth region, are naturally pronounced and can appear unnatural when exaggerated further. Conversely, upper facial expressions for Fear, Sadness, Disgust, and Anger—involving eyebrows, eyelids, and cheeks—are often subtle and hard to perceive, and exaggeration enhances their vividness and realism. Based on these findings, we developed a selective facial expression exaggeration strategy focusing on upper facial features. A validation study with 20 participants confirmed the effectiveness of this approach.
